Market context

Demand in our core market is at record levels, and skilled labor is the constraint. At the same time, our deals are more competitive and AI is redefining what buyers expect, which gives us a limited window to establish our position.

Record backlog, constrained by labor.

The commercial aircraft order backlog is above 17,000 aircraft, close to 12 years of current production. IATA names skilled-labor shortages, especially in engine and component manufacturing, as a constraint on ramp-up and doesn’t expect supply and demand to normalize before 2031–2034 (IATA).

The pressure is inside our accounts.

Boeing ended Q2 2026 with a $715B backlog. GE Aerospace relies on more than 500 direct suppliers, any of which can slow an engine through a labor shortage. At Textron Aviation, about half the workforce has less than five years of experience (GlobalAir). Honeywell Aerospace plans to add more than 1,200 engineering and manufacturing roles this year, and Boeing is increasing its hiring (People Matters, citing Reuters).

Our ICP is widening through accounts we already have.

Neo-primes like Anduril are potential net-new key accounts in A&D. Westinghouse and BWXT open nuclear as part of our ICP, and the United Airlines opportunity does the same for commercial airlines and MRO.

Our top deals are all competitive now.

AG5 and Kahuna show up in our largest deals, and build-your-own is a real alternative, including IT groups using AI to build internal tools.

AI questions are now standard in every deal.

Buyers ask whether we have AI, how we support their own LLMs, and how we secure their data. If we’re seen mainly as raw data that plugs into a broader workforce system, we risk being pigeonholed as an OJT workflow tool rather than a workforce operations system.

SaaS pricing is under scrutiny.

SaaS consolidation and pressure on subscription pricing are well underway in other markets. Ours is behind, but we should expect more scrutiny of how per-seat pricing reflects the value we deliver.

So whatOur customers are hiring inexperienced workers into production ramps they can’t afford to slow. Getting people qualified and deployed faster is a board-level problem for them, which supports the A&D focus. And we have a limited window to establish ourselves as more than a workflow tool, which is the focus of the AI session.

The long view: why $10M matters

$10M of ARR is a milestone, not the end of the road. It’s the point where both how the market values us and what our profits can fund change meaningfully.

It changes how buyers value us.

For strategic and private equity buyers, about $10M is roughly the threshold where sub-scale discounts start to fall away. At that point we can be compared with vertical software businesses in the $10–100M ARR range. An MES buyer, for example, wouldn’t discount our ARR purely for lack of scale. We’d still be small, but big enough to move a strategic buyer’s P&L within one to two years of further growth, if that buyer plans to fold us into a $100–200M line of business.

It changes what we can fund.

At about $4M of ARR, roughly $400K of profit pays for expected variable increases. At $10M, with $1M or more of profit, we can fund meaningful investment in market expansion from our own earnings.

It only pays off if we build intelligence, not just scale.

Industrial buyers are paying a premium, well above common multiples, for proprietary data and intelligence systems. If we can’t both reach scale and build a true intelligence system, we’ll look like a less attractive investment or acquisition target in three to five years.

We chose depth to get there.

We deprioritized Automotive mid-year, moving it from a pillar to an initiative and then lower. A&D tailwinds gave us conviction that $10M is reachable without aggressively opening the OEM market in 2026 or 2027.

Current state: where are we today?

Covy, our implementation agent, shipped to client tenants behind a flag in Q3. It runs inside our app on Bedrock in GovCloud, with permissions checked on every tool call, an approval gate for every write, and full audit records. Its roughly 12 tools are entity-shaped (list, view, create, update), which suits implementation. None are the computed “Reason” tools that answer questions like “who can cover this job?”, and there’s no external MCP access yet. A Claude Gov connector is recommended for Q4, and a GE external-agent beta is coming up.

Usage tells us where AI should start. Capability Management carries the volume, with about 2,000 users a month on sign-offs and about 680 on Start Renewal. IWA had one user in six months.

The main gaps are a draft state and approval inbox inside normal workflows, persona-level data scoping, an event architecture agents can subscribe to, and an eval framework run against a validated customer question set. We’re building with three engineers or fewer, with Scheduling due at the end of November.

Vision: one tool layer, two front doors

We’re also not alone for long. Nobody in frontline workforce software exposes a skills and eligibility graph to agents today: Cornerstone reaches agents through Salesforce’s marketplace, Tulip has an open-source MCP server, and we found nothing from Solumina, Augmentir or UKG. That white space won’t stay open.

2027 commercial: what does AI mean for this year’s plan?

The 2027 growth plan doesn’t depend on AI revenue today. These three questions decide whether it should, and how fast we move.

  1. What are the major hurdles to selling AI to our customers and building it into our sales process?

    Consider data security and ITAR questions in core A&D accounts, AI budgets coming out of existing tech spend, and how ready our team is to position AI against AG5, Kahuna and build-your-own.

  2. How important and how feasible is it for AI to affect our 2027 growth plan?

    Should AI carry its own ARR target, or should we count on it mainly to win and protect deals and lift expansion?

  3. How much validation do we need before any offering moves out of beta?

    The proposed governed beta is two or three customers, read-only, for 90 days, with close criteria agreed up front. What evidence, such as answer quality against our question set, customer usage and willingness to pay, is enough to move to general availability?

What the market tells us (market check as of September 24, 2026; some rates directional):

Credits are the enterprise norm.

Atlassian, Salesforce, Workday and ServiceNow all use credits or metering. Atlassian’s model, with simple MCP reads free and cross-product calls metered, is the closest match to ours.

The market is moving toward metering agent calls.

Salesforce announced charges for agentic API and MCP calls in September 2026.

Outcome pricing has precedent.

Fin charges per resolution, a model for our Shift Cover pilot.

Locking agents out backfires.

SAP drew public pushback for restricting agent access, and JPMorgan called ServiceNow’s metering a tax on outside agents. Both support an open door with free reads.

AI usually comes out of existing budgets.

About 70% of companies say so, so expect AI to be packaged into tiers rather than sold as a new budget line. We’d also avoid default-on overage with manufacturing buyers.

Guiding principles: questions for the room

Each of the four questions below needs an answer by the end of the session. Under each are candidate principles from the AI project work, framed as choices for the room to make.

What are we, and what are we not going to be?

  • Should Covalent own the reasoning, so external agents ask us for answers rather than reasoning outside Covalent and pushing commands in?
  • Is our value the answers only Covalent can calculate, rather than the raw data underneath them?

How do we prioritize?

  • Should we build a vertical agent only when it passes the rubric, and judge progress by that rather than by the number of agents?
  • Should we start where usage already is, in Capability Management?

How do we get to the vision?

  • Do we build only the thin, differentiating parts of the harness and adopt the rest?
  • Do we open a governed door to customers’ own agents now, rather than wait?
  • Should access be open while reasoning is metered: open reads, metered intelligence?

How do we protect this vision?

  • Should agents prepare and people always certify, and should we make that a public commitment?
  • Should every answer show its evidence while our weights and solvers stay private?
  • Can we commit contractually to no model training on customer data and zero retention?
